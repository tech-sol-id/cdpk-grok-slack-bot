Pre-Installation Preparation & Site Survey
Site Survey Essentials
Verify Construction Drawings On Site
Compare two-way radio drawings with actual site conditions, including equipment, antenna locations, signals, grounding, and power. Resolve discrepancies to avoid commissioning failures and coverage dead zones.
Confirm Coverage Requirements
Define coverage areas, user numbers, talk-group allocation, and priority-protection zones.
Equipment-Room Conditions
Confirm equipment-room location, power capacity, grounding, HVAC/heat dissipation, and cable-routing space.
Construction Preparation Checklist
Equipment & materials: repeaters, combiner platforms, OMUs/ORUs, antennas, feeder cables, splitters, and couplers
Tools: diagonal cutters, utility knife, spanners, etc.
Safety equipment: hard hats, insulated gloves, and working-at-height PPE
Documentation: construction drawings, location schedule, and equipment manuals
Permits: construction permit, working-at-height approval, and power-use application
Key reminder: survey data directly determines equipment selection and location design. Always use a field-strength meter for on-site signal testing; do not rely on drawings alone.

Safety Standards
Use safety harnesses for work at height and work in pairs; isolate power before live-work tasks; post warning signs and maintain a safe, orderly site.

Antenna-Feeder System & Equipment Installation Essentials
Antenna-feeder and equipment installation quality directly determines coverage performance. The following are key technical requirements.
Installation Item | Technical Requirements | Common Issues | Acceptance Criteria
Indoor Antenna | Level, downward-facing coverage; >30 cm from metal objects; securely fixed above the ceiling | Tilted installation, too close to metal, or loose fixing causes uneven signal | Levelness <5°
Feeder Cable Routing | Bend radius >10× cable diameter; route horizontally/vertically; uniform tie spacing | Sharp bends, twisting, shared conduit with power cables, or missing labels | No obvious twisting
Connector Assembly | Clean cut; reliable soldering; at least 3 layers of waterproof tape; correct torque | Inadequate waterproofing; center conductor too long/short | VSWR <1.5
Equipment Installation | Arrange cabinet equipment logically, leave ventilation space, and route jumpers/power cables correctly | Knotted/twisted jumpers or unclear identification | Logical layout; clear labels
Passive Component Installation | Secure splitters/couplers to cable trays or walls; clear port labels; waterproof where required | Unsupported components, wrong port connection, missing labels, or no outdoor waterproofing | Securely fixed
Lightning Protection & Grounding | Install surge arresters for outdoor antennas; grounding resistance <4 Ω; ground conductor ≥16 mm² | No surge arrester, poor grounding, or insufficient conductor size | Resistance <4 Ω
Key metric: the antenna-feeder system VSWR must be below 1.5. This is a key installation-quality indicator and must be checked section by section with a VSWR meter.

Two-Way Radio Communications in Enclosed & Semi-Enclosed Spaces
Connector assembly is a millimeter-precision task. Stripping dimensions, crimp torque, and shield treatment are the three essentials.
Remove corrugated shielding: retain 3.5–4.0 mm (insulation must be exposed).
Strip PTFE insulation: expose 2.8–3.2 mm of the center conductor (flat and burr-free).
Cable core dimension: 8–9 mm for the cable core inserted into the connector (core chamfer C0.5–C1).
Procedure: use a staged stripping tool and strip the layers in three steps (armor → shield → insulation).

--- Slide 14 ---
Important notes:
Unreel coaxial cable in the direction of the reel. Do not twist or knot it, as this affects impedance and shielding performance.
The bend radius is generally 6–15× the cable outside diameter (e.g., about 120 mm for RG-213/U) and must never be smaller.
Do not step on, crush, or flatten the cable. Crushing damages the coaxial structure and seriously affects impedance and shielding. Use grommets, rubber sleeves, or equivalent protection at holes and metal edges.
Keep clear of interference sources. Maintain at least 30 cm from power cables (50 cm or more for parallel routing), preferably crossing at right angles. Avoid parallel routing in the same tray. Do not coil cable near operating equipment; neatly coil excess length away from equipment while observing the minimum bend radius.

Digital Fiber Optical Master Unit (OMU) – Core Operating Guide
The illustration shows the OMU local LCD operating interface, displaying real-time operating status, optical-power levels, and key alarms.
During commissioning, monitor parameter changes to ensure stable downlink optical output and accurately configured alarm thresholds for signal-transmission quality.
01 Pre-Commissioning: Safety & Connections
Safety check: ensure reliable grounding (resistance <4 Ω), ≥15 cm ventilation clearance behind the cabinet, and secure power connections.
Physical links: connect N-type connectors to TX/RX RF ports, LC-UPC jumpers to fiber, and RJ45 directly to the commissioning PC.
Tools: commissioning PC with Ethernet port, Gigabit Ethernet cable, and Phillips screwdriver.
02 Commissioning: Configuration & Protection
Authorized login: enter the settings interface and select Chinese or English.
Core adjustment: adjust downlink ATT attenuation to control optical power; configure loss-of-light and over-power alarms.
Lock protection: enable the Engineering Commissioning Lock after setup to prevent accidental changes.

Digital Fiber Optical Remote Unit (ORU) – Core Operating Guide
01 Pre-Commissioning
Safety check: ensure reliable grounding (resistance <4 Ω), ≥15 cm ventilation clearance behind the cabinet, and correct power polarity.
Physical connections:
RF-OUT RF port: confirm the N-type connector is connected to the antenna-feeder network and VSWR is below 1.5.
OPT1–4 fiber ports: confirm correct fiber connection to the OMU.
RJ45 commissioning port: connect the commissioning PC.
Tools: a commissioning PC, straight-through Ethernet cable, Phillips screwdriver, and—when required—optical and RF power meters.
02 Local Panel Commissioning
Device login: enter the settings interface and select Chinese or English.
Core settings:
Adjust uplink ATT attenuation under Real-Time Parameter Settings using ATT +/−.
Configure alarms under Alarm Enable Settings, including no optical receive, uplink power overload, and VSWR alarms.
Lock protection: enable the Engineering Lock after setup to prevent unauthorized changes.
💡 Key reminder: after changing any parameter, use Save Configuration in the interface to prevent loss after power-off.

--- Slide 27 ---
Digital Fiber Optical Remote Unit (ORU) –
Verification, Troubleshooting & Summary
01 Commissioning Verification Criteria
Panel status
No red alarm indication; real-time RF power and optical power display normally.
Functional test
Use a radio to call within the coverage area. Audio must be clear, without dropped calls or noise.
02 Quick Reference: Common Faults
No optical receive alarm: fiber is disconnected/broken or the OMU is not powered on; inspect fiber patch cords and OMU status.
Uplink ALC alarm: uplink input is too strong; increase uplink attenuation.
VSWR alarm: antenna-feeder network fault; inspect the RF-OUT port and antenna-feeder line.
Output under-power: low received optical power or high link loss; check received optical power and reduce downlink attenuation.
03 Key O&M Rules
Safety first; reliable grounding
The equipment must be reliably grounded to prevent electrostatic board damage. Do not work outdoors in thunderstorms.
Adjust parameters carefully; back up first
Back up before configuration changes. Do not increase gain arbitrarily; this can cause network interference or oscillation.
Routine inspection; archive data
Check operating status monthly, record optical power and alarm logs, and maintain a complete equipment lifecycle file.

Common Fault Troubleshooting & Resolution
Fault Symptom | Likely Cause | Troubleshooting Method | Corrective Action
No signal / coverage dead zone | Insufficient antenna locations, excessive feeder loss, or insufficient repeater power | Use a field-strength meter to test each area and map coverage | Add antenna locations or increase power
Noisy / intermittent calls | Poor connector contact, high VSWR, or co-channel interference | Measure VSWR and use a spectrum analyzer to scan interference | Remake connectors or change frequency
Repeater will not start | Power fault, blown fuse, or thermal protection | Use a multimeter to test supply voltage; inspect fuse and cooling fan | Replace power supply/fuse or improve cooling
One-way communication only | Incorrect TX/RX frequency, mismatched CTCSS, or antenna fault | Verify all equipment frequencies and CTCSS parameters; test antenna | Standardize parameter configuration or replace antenna
Cross-talk / channel interference | Poor channel allocation, no CTCSS, or adjacent-channel interference | Analyze spectrum occupancy and check CTCSS settings | Replan channels and set CTCSS
Troubleshooting principle: check power before signal, core equipment before terminals, parameters before hardware, and proceed from simple to complex.
