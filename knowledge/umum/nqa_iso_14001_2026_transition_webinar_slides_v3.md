WHY IS ISO 14001 BEING UPDATED? 
• To better reflect global environmental priorities.
• Strengthen climate action and resilience. 
• Strengthens change management and supply 
chain oversight
• Expands EMS scope with lifecycle perspective, 
supporting focus beyond facilities & operations.
• Support consistent application and better 
alignment with ISO’s Harmonized Structure


--- Halaman 7 ---
OVERVIEW OF THE 2026 REVISION
While the 2026 changes are deemed 
moderate, they will enhance the following: 
• Climate action and environmental resilience.
• Supply chain sustainability and due diligence.
• Lifecycle perspective (from design to disposal).
• Risk-based thinking throughout the 
environmental management systems (EMS).
• Expand Annex A for clearer implementation 
guidance.

--- Halaman 9 ---
GENERAL CHANGES
ISO/FDIS 14001:2026 includes some editorial changes, updating references, 
restructuring, renumbering minor changes and some points of clarification. 
• This includes the use of result as the term used for the output of a process, such as 
management review, or intended outcome of an objective. 
• ‘Outcome’ remains the relevant term when considering the whole EMS.
• Additional terminology changes includes changing the term fulfilment to meeting in 
reference to the compliance obligations.
• Documentation shall now be available rather than maintained.
• Increased references to using a life cycle perspective that prevents adverse 
environmental impacts from being unintentionally shifted elsewhere within the life cycle

--- Halaman 10 ---
CLAUSE 4 UPDATE 
CONTEXT OF THE ORGANISATION
Clause 4.1: 
• Formalises the updates introduced in February 2024. 
• Now explicitly includes environmental conditions, including climate change, pollution, biodiversity, 
natural resource availability and ecosystem health.
• Encourages deeper integration of such environmental factors into wider business context and 
onwards into aspects.
Clause 4.2: 
• Expanded focus on stakeholder requirements again from the February 2024 additions
• Stakeholders may have sustainability requirements (note 1)
• Organisations must review and update stakeholder lists & needs.
• The relevant needs and expectations of interested parties, become a compliance obligation when the 
organisation decides to comply with them.

--- Halaman 11 ---
CLAUSE 4 UPDATE 
CONTEXT OF THE ORGANISATION
Clause 4.3: 
• Scope has to consider the organisation’s authority and ability to exercise control and influence, over 
the life cycle of its activities, products and services. 
• Once the scope is defined, all activities, products and services of the organization within that scope 
shall be included in the environmental management system

--- Halaman 12 ---
CLAUSE 5 UPDATE – LEADERSHIP
Leadership Commitment: 
• New emphasis on ethical leadership, full 
accountability and the fostering of a culture of 
environment  & sustainable development. 
• Leadership has a more visible role in embedding 
sustainability principles into operations.
• Now requires top management to support all 
organisational roles and not just management, 
extending the level of personal involvement, 
responsibility and accountability. 


--- Halaman 13 ---
CLAUSE 5 UPDATE - LEADERSHIP
Clause 5.2: 
• As well as the mandatory commitments (protection of the environment, including prevention of 
pollution) – new note now suggests other specific commitments:
• Sustainable resource usage
• Climate Change mitigation
• Protection of biodiversity & ecosystems

--- Halaman 14 ---
CLAUSE 6 UPDATE 
ACTIONS TO ADDRESS RISK AND OPPORTUNITIES
Clause 6
• Clause 6.1 has been restructured for clarity, with the content of 6.1.1 (general) now moved to 6.1.4.
• ‘Planning actions’ 6.1.4 has been renumbered as 6.1.5. 
• Aspects to include potential emergency situations – change from reasonably foreseeable. Now separated 
from abnormal operations
• Emphasis on the environmental impact across the value chain (as shown by the diagram below): 
Design  Procurement          Production             Use        Disposal

--- Halaman 15 ---
CLAUSE 6.3 UPDATE – NEW CLAUSE COVERING 
CHANGE MANAGEMENT
Clause 6.3: 
• New clause introduced in ISO 14001:2026. 
• Established a planned structured approach in 
managing environmental change, which 
addresses: 
• Compliance obligation changes. 
• Operational adjustments. 
• Mergers, acquisitions, joint ventures etc
• Technological or process shifts
• Business disruption due to supply chain issues, 
labour disputes, natural disasters, regime 
change, political unrest or war!
• It is similar to the approach utilised in the      
ISO 9001:2015 standard.

--- Halaman 17 ---
CLAUSE 8 – OPERATIONS
Requirements for climate adaptation, supply chain 
disruptions and emergency planning. 
• Operational controls strengthened (link to clause 6) 
to reflect supply chain sustainability – outsourced 
processes changed to control of “externally provided 
processes, products and services”
• Lifecycle perspective embedded throughout 
operational planning. 
• Emergency situations to be identified when 
considering ‘risks and opportunities’ and not just 
environmental aspects, so that all eventualities, or 
potential situations are identified including 
changed/new activities. 


--- Halaman 18 ---
CLAUSE 8 
EXTERNALLY PROVIDED PROCESSES, PRODUCTS AND SERVICES
Clarification in Annex A:
The term “outsource” has been deleted and the phrase 
“outsourced processes” has been replaced by “externally 
provided processes, products, or services”
These are:
• used by the organisation but supplied by others outside of the 
organisation
• The phrase “external provider” means an external supplier 
organisation including a contractor that provides a product or a 
service.


--- Halaman 19 ---
CLAUSE 9.2 – INTERNAL AUDITING
Clause 9.2: 
• The effectiveness of the environmental 
management system is fundamental to the whole of 
Clause 9 and is now included in the first sentence 
• New requirement expected that each internal audit 
shall have defined objective(s)
• More detailed documentation to be made available:
• the audit programme
• evidence of the implementation of the audit programme
• evidence of the audit results


--- Halaman 20 ---
CLAUSE 9.3 – MANAGEMENT REVIEW
Clause 9.3: 
• No changes to actual requirements
• Clause 9.3 has been reorganised into two sub-clauses: 
• Inputs. 
• Results.
• Information requirements for the management review are 
now more definitive and 'shall' be included. The phrase 
‘consideration of’ has been dropped.
• This is to improve structure and traceability. 


--- Halaman 21 ---
CLAUSE 10 – IMPROVEMENT
Clause 10: 
• Clause 10.3 has been removed, with its 
content integrated into Clauses 10.1 and 
10.2 
• No other changes. 


--- Halaman 22 ---
KEY TERMINOLOGY AND OTHER CHANGES
Terminology: 
• Terminology updates include replacing “fulfil” with “meets compliance obligations” to align to modern 
regulatory language. 
Executive Engagement: 
• It also places greater emphasis on conserving natural resources and protecting ecosystems, requiring 
organisations to revise their environmental policies and ensure executive engagement. 
• Annex A provides expanded guidance to clarify these requirements, helping businesses navigate the 
changes more effectively. 

--- Halaman 23 ---
PREPARING FOR THE TRANSITION 
Preparing for the Transition: 
• Review your EMS context to include climate and supply chain considerations.
• Align environmental policies with the revised standard’s focus on sustainability.
• Update stakeholder and risk registers.
• Review lifecycle assessment practices.
• Refresh internal audit and management review processes.
• Engage leadership early. 

--- Halaman 24 ---
PREPARING FOR THE TRANSITION (Cont.)
Certification Body
• Keep in close contact with your certification body (NQA) who will offer information and support for this 
transition process. 
• They will ensure that all certified organisation maintain their certification and use the revised standard to 
enhance their sustainability credentials. 
By adopting these changes, organisations can position themselves as leaders in environmental stewardship, 
meet stakeholder expectations and drive long-term value in an increasingly sustainability-focused world.

--- Halaman 25 ---
Summary
• A strengthened focus on a broader range of environmental conditions in addition to climate change for 
the analysis of the organisational context, such as pollution levels, biodiversity and availability of 
natural resources
• Restructured and clarified requirements related to risk and opportunity processes
• New clause and requirements for the planning of changes
• Strengthened emphasis on life cycle perspective in the environmental aspect process
• For operational controls, the focus is extended from “outsourced processes” to “externally provided 
processes, products and services”
• In addition, Annex A (informative), has been substantially extended and improved for various clauses 
to support interpretation of the various requirements.
