Navigating Changes in Intrinsic Safety 
Standards: IEC 60079-11 Edition 7 
The IEC 60079-11 standard covers requirements for electrical equipment designated for 
use in explosive atmospheres incorporating the intrinsic safety method of protection. 
Edition 7 of the standard was published in January 2023, and is considered a technical 
revision of Edition 6 (2011). As such, the seventh edition cancels and replaces the sixth 
edition. The key changes, which are outlined in the Forward of Edition 7, include:
Major technical 
changes 
In total, there are 45 major technical 
changes in Edition 7—which 
include either additional technical 
requirements, or an increase of 
technical requirements, compared 
to Edition 6. 
Extensions 
There are 76 extensions in the 
new standard—that is, additional 
options that add or modify technical 
requirements in a manner that 
would not affect compliance of 
equipment already meeting the 
previous edition of the standard.
Minor or editorial 
changes 
There are 52 of these changes in 
Edition 7, which include principle 
clarifications, minor technical 
changes, and a decrease 
of requirements or editorial 
corrections.  
In this guide, we’ll outline some of the major technical changes that may impact  
manufacturers of electrical equipment designated for use in explosive atmospheres.

Spark test apparatus and its use
Clause Requirement Outcome
9.1.2 Type 
verifications  
and type tests 
“When testing inductors, the effects of service 
temperature on coil resistance shall be 
considered to ensure that the worst-case current 
is flowing through the inductor. Self-heating 
effects during the test shall be ignored. ”
Effect of service temperatures, like relevant 
influence of the low ambient temperature in 
reducing inductor resistance, needs to be 
considered in spark testing assessments. 
Existing certified equipment must be verified 
to confirm compliance in line with this 
new requirement and reassessment of the 
inductive energy may be necessary.

Tests for cells, batteries and supercapacitors
Clause Requirement Outcome
9.14.1.1 – 9.14.1.3 
Tests for cells, 
batteries and 
supercapacitors  
Electrolyte leakage and surface temperature 
test requirements have been modified, as have 
requirements around supercapacitors cells and 
batteries (including removal or bypassing current 
limiting devices of the batteries).
There are major modifications 
incorporated in the section of testing 
supercapacitors, cells, and batteries 
under short circuit conditions. All 
previously certified equipment 
incorporating cells, batteries, or 
supercapacitors must be reviewed, 
reassessed, and retested as necessary, 
in alignment with the new requirements. 
Equipment redesign and revision of 
components incorporated (i.e. cells, 
batteries, or supercapacitors) might be 
required as a result of this change.
Spark ignition assessment
Clause Requirement Outcome
9.14.3.2 Tests 
for cells, 
batteries and 
supercapacitors 
“Where the apparatus contains one or more cells that 
shall not be changed in the explosive atmosphere, the 
spark ignition discharge at the terminals of a single 
cell does not require to be tested, provided that the 
single cell delivers a peak open circuit voltage of less 
than 4,5 V except for supercapacitors and lithium-ion 
cells and batteries with a high short circuit current 
where spark ignition risk should be considered. ”
In the previous edition of the standard, 
spark ignition testing at terminals of 
a single cell rated 4.5V or less was 
not required regardless of the type of 
cell. In Edition 7, it is outlined that for 
supercapacitors and lithium-ion cells 
with a high short circuit capability the 
ignition risk must be assessed regardless 
of whether their voltage is below 4.5V.
As a result, assessments of existing 
certified equipment that include 
supercapacitors and cells need to be 
revisited and, where necessary, the risk 
of spark ignition must be reassessed 
accordingly.
Routine Tests
Clause Requirement Outcome
10.3.1 Routine 
tests for 
transformers 
Routine tests for transformers have changed to also 
include routine tests between windings of separate 
intrinsically safe circuits. The voltage values applicable 
to routine dielectric strength tests are outlined in  
Table 26. 
Edition 7 now specifically outlines how 
to conduct routine dielectric strength 
tests between transformer windings 
separating different intrinsically safe 
circuits. As such, previously certified 
equipment needs to be verified and, 
where found applicable, the respective 
routine test must be implemented 
accordingly in alignment with the  
new requirement.

IEC 60079-11 EDITION 7
All new products that are currently 
preparing for certification should be 
designed and evaluated in accordance 
with Edition 7. 
At this time, products that are already 
certified do not need to comply with 
Edition 7, but this may change in the  
future. While effective dates will vary 
among certification schemes, geographies, 
and certification bodies, manufacturers 
can prepare by:
 • Reviewing existing files against IEC 60079-11 
Edition 7 to determine compliance status with the 
new requirements;
 • Ascertaining whether the certification scheme 
requires an update of the file (and contacting 
a certification body for support if it is unclear 
whether the product complies with the new 
requirements); and
 • Contacting a certification body to request an 
upgrade in instances where changes are needed 
to maintain compliance.
