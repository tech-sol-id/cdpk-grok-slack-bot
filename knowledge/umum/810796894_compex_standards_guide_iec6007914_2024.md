oil and gas, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, food manufacturing and utilities.

3. Introduction  
This technical guidance document, developed by the subject matter experts at CompEx, gives you a 
valuable head start in how to efficiently navigate the new standard. The objective of this guidance is to 
summarise the most noteworthy changes and includes an  easy-to-follow mapping section that helps 
cross-reference clauses from Edition 5, which may now be located in any one of the three new sections 
and 17 Annexes of Edition 6. 
The main sections of the new standard are now structured as follows: 
• Design   Clause 6 
• Selection   Clause 7 
• Installation of equipment Clause 8 
• Initial inspection  Clause 91 
The annexes of the new standard are structured as follows: 
• Normative annexes Mandatory (to be treated as any other part of the standard) 
• Informative annexes Not mandatory but offer insightful guidance

4. Background 
The sixth edition of IEC 60079-14:2024 introduces several significant changes in comparison to the 2013 
fifth edition.  
This revision marks a substantial restructuring of the standard, and will be a considerable change to those 
familiar with previous editions. Navigating between the old and new editions will likely be challenging for 
many, as the familiar structure has now been replaced by three distinct sections: 
• Design 
• Selection 
• Installation of equipment 
The title has changed to ‘IEC 60079-14:2024: Electrical Installation Design, Selection and Installation of 
Equipment, Including Initial Inspection’ - emphasising the importance of initial inspections. 
Supplemented by a completely revamped and updated series of annexes, this standard has been 
thoroughly deconstructed and reassembled, section by section, leaving many users struggling to locate 
the clauses they were familiar with in Edition 5. This is a change in approach never seen before in previous 
editions. 
5. Key Updates to IEC 60079-14 
These changes reflect a comprehensive update of IEC 60079-14. The most notable change is the complete 
restructuring of the standard. This new layout introduces a lifecycle-based approach, meaning users need 
to navigate through multiple sections to find requirements that were previously located in one place. This 
is more aligned with industry roles safety considerations. 
5.1 New Terminology and Expanded Scope 
The standard has replaced the term ‘Electrical Equipment ’ with ‘Ex Equipment ’ and applies to fixed, 
transportable, portable and personal, and installations permanent or temporary. Other changes include 
a reference to documentation and personnel competency as well as the relocation of some elements from 
the scope to other clauses within the standard. 
5.2 Summary of Key Changes and Requirements 
The information provided in this section gives a summary of changes to specific clauses and details the 
nature of the update and requirements: 
• Clause 4.3.2 - Static electricity: The guidance on managing static has largely been removed from 
Edition 6, referring the reader instead to IEC TS 60079-32-1. 
• Clause 4.3.6 - Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) tags: RFID tags are now subject to more stringent 
analysis often requiring certification, reflecting the growing complexity and usage of such technology. 
• Clause 6.7 - Electrical machines: This clause has been reformatted for clarity and conciseness. 
• Clause 7.3.2 - External influences: This clause now includes an expanded clause for consideration of 
external influences such as high humidity, spray from waves, powder snow, and sandstorms in 
addition to those already listed in Edition 5. 
• Clause 7.3.8.2  - Considerations for using intrinsic safety with combus tible dust: New guidance is 
provided in which, subject to certain requirements, permits the use of Group II Intrinsically Safe (IS)

the hazardous areas boundary. 
• Annex Q - Simple apparatus: The definition of ‘Simple Apparatus’ has been updated to align with IEC
